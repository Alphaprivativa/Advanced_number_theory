Invertibility is the Weil–Cartier correspondence

Invertibility of ideals in Dedekind domains is the arithmetic form of Correspondence of Weil and Cartier divisors under non singularity assumption:

  • a Cartier divisor is one that is locally principal; an invertible fractional ideal is one that is locally principal at every prime
  • the corollary on a nonsingular (or locally factorial) variety says exactly that every Weil divisor is Cartier, i.e. that every fractional ideal is invertible

In both proofs the same hypothesis does the work: nonsingularity on one side, integral closedness on the other, deployed through the determinant trick to forbid .

Singular curves and non-maximal orders

Drop integral closedness, geometrically, allow to be singular, and the correspondence breaks in a precise way: not every ideal is invertible, , and unique factorization into primes fails.

This is not a pathology but the situation of the Form class group: for a non-fundamental discriminant the relevant ring is the order , which is not integrally closed. Its class group is defined using only the invertible fractional ideals, the exact analogue of using rather than on a singular curve. See The form class group as a Picard group.
