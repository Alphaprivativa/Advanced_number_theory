Abstract

The whole correspondence in one line

An ideal is a lattice, its norm form is a quadratic form of discriminant , dividing by normalizes the discriminant back to , and the two remaining ambiguities, the basis and the scaling of the lattice, are exactly proper equivalence and narrow equivalence. Nothing else happens: the theorem is the observation that these two ambiguities match.

Important

By the geometric dictionary is the Picard group of the arithmetic curve ; for a curve over an algebraically closed field the analogous group is the Picard variety, positive dimensional and never finite.
