# Integral closedness is equivalent to all localizations being DVRs

> Let
> - $A$ a [[Anello Noetheriano|Noetherian]] [[Local ring algebraic|Local]] [[Dominio di integrità|domain]] of [[Krull dimension]] $1$ 
> - maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$
> - [[Campo delle frazioni|field of fractions]] $\mathbb K$
>
> Then the following are equivalent
> 1. $A$ is [[Integral element|integrally closed]]
> 2. $\mathfrak m$ is principal
> 3. $A$ is a [[Regular local ring|discrete valuation ring]]
>
> > [!dim]- #### Proof
> > - $(1) \Longrightarrow (2):$ since $A$ is a Noetherian domain and $\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak m=\dim A=1$, the [[Invertibility of ideals in Dedekind domains#Lemma ( inverse of prime ideals )|lemma on inverses of height-one prime ideals]] gives
> >   $$y\in\mathfrak m^{-1}\setminus A, \qquad y\mathfrak m\subseteq A.$$
> >   Thus $y\mathfrak m$ is an ideal of $A$. It is not contained in $\mathfrak m$: otherwise $\mathfrak m$ would be a faithful finitely generated $A$-module stable under $y$, and [[Characterization of integral elements via finitely generated modules|the determinant trick]] would make $y$ integral over $A$, hence an element of $A$ by $(1)$, a contradiction. Being an ideal of a local ring escaping $\mathfrak m$, it is the whole ring, and $y\mathfrak m=A$ gives $\mathfrak m=y^{-1}A$.
> >   
> >   It remains only to show that $y^{-1}\in A$, but this is true because $y\mathfrak m=A$ so $ym=1$ for $m \in \mathfrak m$, so $m=y^{-1}.$
> > - $(2) \Longrightarrow (3):$ a local Noetherian ring of dimension $1$ whose maximal ideal is generated by $1 = \dim A$ element is [[Regular local ring|regular of dimension 1]], which is the definition of a discrete valuation ring.
> > - $(3) \Longrightarrow (1):$ write $\mathfrak m = (t)$. By the [[Krull Intersection Theorem]] $\bigcap_n \mathfrak m^n = 0$, so every $a \ne 0$ has a largest $n$ with $a \in (t^n)$, and $a = ut^n$ with $u \notin \mathfrak m$ a unit. Hence every non-zero ideal is some $(t^n)$, $A$ is a [[Dominio a ideali principali|PID]], and [[Every UFD is integrally closed|a UFD is integrally closed]].
>
> > [!important]
> > In $(1) \Longrightarrow (2)$, ==integral closedness is spent== in the determinant trick, while $\dim A=1$ lets us apply the lemma to $\mathfrak m$. The implication $(2) \Longrightarrow (3)$ uses $\dim A=1$ to identify regularity, and $(3) \Longrightarrow (1)$ uses the valuation structure of a DVR to obtain a PID.
>
> > [!cor] ### Corollary (Integrally closed equals locally a DVR)
> > Let $A$ be a [[Anello Noetheriano|Noetherian]] [[Dominio di integrità|domain]] of [[Krull dimension]] $1$. Then
> > $$A \text{ integrally closed} \iff A_{\mathfrak p} \text{ is a DVR for every non-zero prime } \mathfrak p \le A$$
> >
> > > [!dim]- #### Proof
> > > - $\Longrightarrow:$ localization preserves integral closedness. If $z \in \mathbb K$ satisfies a monic relation of degree $d$ over $A_{\mathfrak p}$ and $s \notin \mathfrak p$ clears all its denominators, multiplying by $s^d$ makes $sz$ integral over $A$, hence $sz \in A$ and $z \in A_{\mathfrak p}$. Moreover $\dim A_{\mathfrak p}= \operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p = 1$, since $(0)\subsetneq\mathfrak p$ in a domain of dimension $1$, so the theorem applies to $A_{\mathfrak p}$.
> > > - $\Longleftarrow:$ first $A = \bigcap_{\mathfrak m} A_{\mathfrak m}$ inside $\mathbb K$, the intersection over the maximal ideals: for $z$ in every $A_{\mathfrak m}$ the ideal of denominators $\{c \in A \mid cz \in A\}$ lies in no maximal ideal, hence is $A$, hence contains $1$. Now a $z \in \mathbb K$ integral over $A$ is integral over each $A_{\mathfrak m}$, which is a DVR and so integrally closed by the theorem, so $z$ lies in every $A_{\mathfrak m}$ and therefore in $A$.
>
> > [!note]
> > This is the bridge between the two definitions of a [[Dedekind domain]], the arithmetic one asking for integral closedness and the local-algebra one asking that every localization be regular. Read geometrically it says that on a curve ==normal and regular are the same condition==, which is what [[Dedekind domains are the smooth affine curves]] turns into a statement about singularities.
