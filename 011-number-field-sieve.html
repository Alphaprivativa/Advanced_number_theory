How the algebraic square root is computed

Step (4) leaves the algorithm for unspecified. Two standard approaches are:

  1. Polynomial factorization over the number field. Factor . A linear factor supplies the root; irreducibility proves that no root exists in . See Stevenhagen, The Number Field Sieve, §10.
  2. Finite-field roots, Hensel lifting, and reconstruction. Reduce at a suitable auxiliary prime, find a square root in the residue field, and lift it to increasing prime-power precision. With coefficient and denominator bounds, reconstruct a candidate in and verify exactly. In the simplest version stays irreducible modulo the auxiliary prime; more general versions handle several residue-field factors and their root choices. See Thomé, The square root step, slides 19–25.

The lifting approach is also a technique underlying number-field polynomial factorization. A root modulo an auxiliary prime alone does not certify a root in ; exact reconstruction and verification are essential. In the Quadratic sieve, Tonelli–Shanks instead locates the sieve progressions, while the final integer square root comes from halving the known prime exponents.
